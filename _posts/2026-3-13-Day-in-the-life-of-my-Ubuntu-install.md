Welcome to my Ubuntu install. I suppose you'd like to boot your computer. Use the handy flowchart below to determine your boot process. (Note: you will need a 6-sided die, and if no directions are provided, continue to the next number.)

1. Turn on or restart the computer. Immediately start spamming the F12 key.
2. At the boot menu, see if the USB is showing up as bootable. To determine this, roll your die once. If you roll above a 4, it shows up. If it shows up, go to 3. Otherwise, go to 1.
3. At GRUB, select "Ubuntu" (the top option).
4. After the boot logo, determine if you booted to a BusyBox shell, or GNOME. Roll your die once. If you roll a 3 or less, you boot to a shell. Go to 5. Otherwise, go to 6.
5. Type "exit" a few times. If a bootup log shows up, continue to 6. Otherwise, go to 1.
6. Log in.
7. Roll your die once. If you roll a one, at some point the disk stops reading and writing and your session abruptly breaks down. When that happens, go to 1. If you rolled a 2 or higher, proceed to 8.
8. Congratulations! You have now booted my Ubuntu install!

In all seriousness, though, this is pretty similar to how it feels to use it. What rational person would live with this? I would, but I'm not rational. Still better than Windows, though! ;)
