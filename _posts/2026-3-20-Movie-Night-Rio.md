## _Rio_ (2011) is about species conservation, love, and... _gazebos?_
<!-- end-exc -->
_Rio_ follows Blu and Jewel, who are the last individuals of their specific species of parrot. They start out hating each other and, well, we've all seen this kind of movie before. They spend the movie running around the city, chased by exotic animal smugglers.

It's a fun movie, and that comes with everything it implies. It doesn't take itself too seriously, and as a result you get a funny film with only a few plot holes. It's not trying to be a Christopher Nolan film, and it's not. But for what it is, it's an entertaining kids' movie starring two likable parrots.

And let's talk about those parrots for a minute. They're stand-ins for humans, as animals often are in films - after all, movies not about relatable human emotions aren't easy to become attached to. So while these might not be the only unrealistic animals in film, they aren't exactly scientific. One early example of this is when Blu is standing on a windowsill, enjoying hot cocoa and cookies. This is fairly heartwarming and cute, but _parrots can't have chocolate._ Multiple times throughout the movie I found myself saying "That's not how birds work!". They do look nice, however, which brings me to my next point.

_Rio_ is a beautiful movie. While 15 whole years old at the time that I'm writing this, I was impressed multiple times by how the visuals largely hold up even today. Now, if a major studio came out with a movie with _Rio_'s visuals today, it would be a different story. But the birds' feathers and the landscapes are truly striking - maybe even the film's greatest attribute.

There's one more thing I haven't touched on yet: What do I mean about gazebos? The movie doesn't prominently feature any, so what could I possibly be talking about? Well, one of the film's recurring songs features the lyric, "then we can chill in a gazebo". What does this mean? I don't know. What I _do_ know is that my least favorite part of the movie was definitely the music. The songs are just kind of cringe, and I can't really explain why.

All in all, _Rio_ is a fun, pretty film that you can enjoy if you're not looking for an overly interesting plot. It largely holds up today, and I'm overall happy I watched it.

### 3.5/5
